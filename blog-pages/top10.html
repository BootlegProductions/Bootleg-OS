10 Useful Web Tools to Simplify Your Life

Ten corners of the internet I regularly return to for research, editing, building websites and finding the material behind an idea.

In this postThese are not necessarily the ten most technically advanced websites on the internet. They are tools and categories that have repeatedly helped me research subjects, produce graphics, edit videos, find obscure clips and build the projects featured throughout my portfolio.

#10 — Artificial intelligence

Artificial intelligence is potentially the most controversial entry on this list.

For a large part of the population, genuinely capable AI existed almost entirely as science-fiction fantasy. It belonged to films about computers becoming self-aware, robots taking over the world or somebody giving a machine one poorly worded instruction and accidentally causing the extinction of humanity.

That technology is now developing in public, practically in real time, while many people still have very little understanding of what it means or where it may eventually lead. That larger conversation deserves an entire article of its own.

AI has already been integrated into search engines, mobile phones, image editors, office software, customer-service systems and practically every other corner of technology. Sometimes it provides a genuinely useful feature. Other times it feels as though the term has been attached to something because every company has decided that its product must contain AI whether anybody asked for it or not.

I do not believe it needs to be incorporated into everything.

At its best, I see AI as the next advanced step in search and assistance. It can operate as a translator, calculator, writing aid, coding assistant and research tool at the same time. Instead of returning ten pages that may contain an answer somewhere, it can attempt to gather the relevant information and explain it directly.

In that sense, it is a conglomeration of several older tools rolled into one enormous prediction machine—a digital Magic 8 Ball attempting to pull answers through a shroud of ones and zeroes.

However, “prediction” is the important word. AI does not possess a perfect encyclopaedia of unquestionable truth. It generates responses based on patterns and available information, which means it can misunderstand a request, repeat human bias or confidently invent something that sounds completely believable.

It is useful, but it is not always the answer to every problem.

I have found it helpful for developing code, organising ideas, troubleshooting projects and approaching a problem from another direction. It can explain an unfamiliar concept or help identify the likely cause of an error. Even then, its work needs to be tested and judged rather than accepted automatically.

Its potential for misuse is enormous, from misinformation and impersonation to replacing human work with cheap imitations. Science fiction has spent decades offering warnings about poorly controlled technology, and it would be foolish to assume that none of those concerns matter simply because the current version can help write an email.

Used carefully, AI can be an extremely flexible tool. Used lazily, it becomes a machine that does the thinking for somebody who has stopped checking whether its answer makes any sense.

#9 — Video downloaders

Video downloaders are exceptionally useful tools, particularly for editors, researchers and anybody attempting to preserve something that may not remain online forever.

There are browser-based downloaders, desktop applications and command-line programs capable of saving videos from various platforms. I am not linking directly to individual downloading sites because they frequently change address, become filled with misleading advertisements or disappear altogether.

They are generally not difficult to find, but they should be approached carefully.

I use downloaded clips as source material for editing. My YouTube intros often combine scenes from cartoons and television with Fallout: New Vegas backgrounds, characters and objects. Having the clip available locally allows me to cut it precisely, remove frames, separate the audio and composite elements without depending on an internet connection every time I reopen the project.

Downloaders can also be useful for archiving interviews, news footage, obscure uploads, deleted scenes and funny moments that may eventually be removed. Online video feels permanent until a channel closes, a copyright claim appears, an account is deleted or the platform decides that an old upload is no longer worth hosting.

These tools are constantly being blocked, shut down, purchased or drowned beneath imitations. Platforms understandably want to protect copyrighted material and keep viewing inside their own services, but the result can also make legitimate personal archiving unnecessarily difficult.

Even a paid offline-download feature may keep the video locked inside an application rather than giving the user an ordinary file they can edit, catalogue or transfer. At that point, screen recording can sometimes achieve the same result with additional inconvenience and reduced quality.

There are still copyright considerations. A tool being capable of downloading something does not automatically provide permission to redistribute it. The safest uses involve your own uploads, public-domain material, openly licensed work or clips used within the boundaries of applicable quotation, criticism and fair-dealing rules.

The downloader itself is simply a tool. What matters is the source material and what somebody does with the copy afterwards.

#8 — Background-removal tools

Useful linksremove.bg · Canva Video Background Remover

Background-removal tools have become an essential part of my editing and graphic-design workflow.

They identify the main subject of an image or video, separate it from the surrounding background and create a transparent cut-out. That object can then be placed into an entirely different composition.

I use this process constantly when creating thumbnails and edited videos. A character can be removed from a cartoon, placed into a Fallout location and surrounded by objects from the game. A person photographed against an ordinary wall can be transferred into a poster design. Individual parts of an illustration can be separated and reused as editable assets.

remove.bg is one of the best-known browser-based examples. It can automatically create transparent PNGs and includes tools for correcting areas that were removed or retained incorrectly. It also offers integrations and applications beyond the browser version.

Video removal is more demanding because the tool has to continue identifying the subject across every frame. Unscreen previously offered a simple dedicated service for this, but its address now redirects to Canva’s Video Background Remover, which is offered as a Canva Pro feature.

These services are not always perfect. Hair, smoke, transparent objects, motion blur and subjects with colours similar to the background can confuse the detection. Free versions may restrict resolution, video length or the number of daily exports, while the cleanest results are often placed behind a subscription.

That is one reason I have also been developing my own browser-based background-removal tool for Bootleg OS. My version combines automatic subject detection with colour-key removal and manual erase-and-restore brushes. It has taken considerable testing to improve the edge detection, preserve linework and provide visible brush outlines so the user can see exactly which area is being edited.

Building one myself has also given me a much better understanding of how difficult the apparently simple instruction “remove the background” actually is.

#7 — Green Screen Memes

Useful linksgreenscreenmemes.com

Green Screen Memes provides a large catalogue of short clips that have already been isolated and placed over a green background.

The collection includes internet memes, viral clips and scenes from television, films and online videos. Instead of manually cutting a person from every frame, an editor can download an existing green-screen version and remove the colour using chroma key.

The basic process is simple:

  1. Find a suitable clip.
  2. Download and import it into your editing software.
  3. Select the green background with the chroma-key tool.
  4. Adjust the threshold and edge settings.
  5. Place the remaining subject over your own footage.

This is especially useful for quick edits where the recognisable clip is more important than achieving a flawless cinematic mask.

A good green-screen source can save an enormous amount of time, but the results still depend on the original preparation. Compression, green reflections, motion blur and rough edges may require additional correction. A subject wearing green can also lose parts of their clothing or body when the key is applied.

It is also worth remembering that removing a clip’s background does not remove its original copyright. These clips are tools for editing and transformation, not automatically copyright-free footage.

The site is useful because it turns a process that might otherwise require frame-by-frame cutting into something that can be assembled in minutes.

#6 — Yarn

Useful linksYarn

Yarn is one of the most underrated tools I use for video editing.

It allows users to search dialogue from films and television programmes and find short clips containing the words or phrases they entered. If I remember part of a quote but cannot remember the episode, or need a character saying something very specific, Yarn can often locate it.

That is useful even if you are not editing anything. Somebody might remember a line from a sitcom, recognise the voice in their head and have absolutely no idea where it came from. Searching the quotation can identify the programme and scene much faster than attempting to describe it to a normal search engine.

For my Fallout: New Vegas videos, I use Yarn to search for dialogue or situations that parallel events in the game. Terms such as “the desert,” “radiation,” “overloaded,” “Roman Empire,” “monster,” “water” or “we’re going to die” can lead to scenes that might become an episode intro.

The best results are not always an exact reference to Fallout. Sometimes a character simply describes a situation that can be re-edited into the Mojave. I can remove them from the original scene, replace the background and add creatures or items from the game.

Yarn is particularly useful because searching the dialogue creates a different route into film and television than searching by title. I may discover a scene from something I had never considered using simply because one sentence fits the edit.

Its library does not contain everything, and searches can produce many unrelated results, but when it finds the right clip it can save hours of manually searching through episodes.

#5 — Website builders

Useful linksWix · WordPress

Website builders such as Wix and WordPress have allowed almost anybody to create a blog, shop, charity page, business website or portfolio without learning how to code everything from the beginning.

I was taught to use some of these platforms during university, which felt slightly strange while studying Computer Science and Web Design. After learning HTML, CSS, JavaScript and development principles, being shown how to drag pre-built sections into a template could feel like an expensive lesson in something designed to be usable by almost anyone.

The systems deliberately guide users through the process. Hosting, mobile layouts, themes, contact forms and shop features can be included without needing to construct all of the underlying technology manually.

That simplicity is both their greatest advantage and their main weakness.

Template-built websites can begin to look and behave very similarly. The same layouts, hero banners, button styles and page structures appear repeatedly. More unusual customisation may require paid plans, additional plugins or fighting against the restrictions of the template.

For my own projects, I usually prefer the control available through writing the site myself. However, I can also see where website builders are genuinely valuable.

Small local businesses often do not need an experimental interactive website. They need a clear page containing their opening hours, location, contact information, products and perhaps a simple online shop. A builder can get that site online quickly and allow the business owner to update it without calling a developer whenever one photograph or price changes.

I encountered this while helping with my aunt’s Sweet Shop website. She had already begun the site through Wix, and I could work from that foundation, improve its images and information and make it more useful without creating an entirely new content-management system she would then have to learn.

Website builders may not offer unlimited freedom, but they reduce the barrier between having an idea and giving that idea a functioning place online.

#4 — Stock image libraries

Useful linksPixabay · Pexels · Shutterstock

Stock-image websites provide photographs, illustrations, video clips and other assets that can be used in designs without relying on whatever low-resolution result happens to appear first in Google Images.

I use stock material for backgrounds, mock-ups, website banners, video edits and visual references. When a project needs a desert landscape, CRT television, cloudy sky, city skyline or generic office scene, a stock library can provide a clean, high-resolution starting point.

Pixabay and Pexels offer large collections of freely accessible images and videos, while Shutterstock contains a much broader commercial library built around licensing and subscription plans.

The main limitation is that stock images can feel like stock images.

The most popular photographs appear across hundreds of websites, adverts and presentation templates. Search results can be dominated by polished but oddly emotionless people pointing at blank spaces, laughing at salads or conducting business meetings in rooms no human has ever actually worked in.

Free libraries may also return a narrower selection than paid collections. It sometimes takes additional search terms, several different websites and a bit of gumption to find something that does not look completely generic.

Licences should always be checked, even on services described as free. Rules can vary around redistribution, trademarks, recognisable people and selling an unmodified asset as a product.

Used well, stock imagery is raw material rather than the finished design. Cropping, colour correction, layering and combining it with original assets can turn a common photograph into something specific to the project.

#3 — Image editors

Useful linksCanva · Photopea · GIMP

Canva is one of the most accessible and widely used image-editing platforms, largely because it combines a large number of functions inside a relatively simple interface.

I have used it for several years to create YouTube thumbnails, posters, logos, website graphics, social-media images and composited assets. It can also handle basic video, animation, presentations and a variety of export formats.

Each design type comes with templates, typefaces, shapes, stickers, photographs and other assets that can be placed directly onto the canvas. Many are available without separate attribution, although users still need to pay attention to whether an element is free or included only with a premium plan.

That distinction is one of my largest frustrations with Canva.

It can offer temporary access to premium features, allow one premium object to slip into a design or make a tool appear available until the point of export. After spending time building something, the user can suddenly discover that downloading it requires a trial or subscription.

Despite that annoyance, the free version remains capable enough to produce a large amount of useful work. My YouTube thumbnail process combines Canva with background-removal services and individually prepared assets. I still follow a template developed when I began the channel, but the results have gradually become cleaner and more defined as I have learned more.

Canva is not the only option. Photopea provides a surprisingly capable Photoshop-style editor inside a browser, while GIMP offers a free desktop application with more advanced manual control.

Different editors suit different jobs. Canva is quick and convenient for layouts and repeatable graphics. Photopea and GIMP provide more direct control over selections, layers and detailed image manipulation.

The most useful editor is generally the one you understand well enough to finish the work.

#2 — Wikis

Useful linksWikipedia · Fandom · Know Your Meme

Wikipedia is the godfather of online reference material: an enormous, constantly evolving encyclopaedia maintained through open collaboration.

It is often the first place people visit when trying to understand a subject. An article can provide the basic history, key terminology and sources needed to continue researching elsewhere.

It should not always be treated as the final unquestionable source, particularly for controversial or rapidly changing subjects. However, dismissing Wikipedia entirely ignores how useful it is as a map. Even when the article itself is only a starting point, its citations can lead towards books, research papers, interviews and primary documents.

Beyond Wikipedia, there are thousands of specialist wikis maintained by particular communities.

Game wikis document quests, items, characters, locations, unused dialogue and technical problems in extraordinary detail. The different Fallout wikis have been invaluable while building my Pi-Boy and producing my Fallout: New Vegas videos. I have used them to check perk information, SPECIAL attributes, item names, artwork, quest details and the relationships between characters.

Fan-maintained databases can sometimes preserve information that the original developer never organised publicly.

Know Your Meme performs a similar job for internet culture. It catalogues memes, traces their origins, records variations and explains jokes that would otherwise become incomprehensible once separated from the week in which everybody was posting them.

Specialist communities also document television series, films, music, hardware, software and practically any other subject somebody has cared enough to catalogue.

Wikis are useful because knowledge does not need to wait for one official organisation to decide it deserves an archive. A group of dedicated people can build one themselves.

#1 — Digital archives

Useful linksInternet Archive · Wayback Machine · Wikimedia Commons

Digital archives are the motherlode of old, forgotten, abandoned, banned and extremely niche media.

They contain scanned books, audio recordings, films, television broadcasts, software, games, magazines, photographs, concert recordings, government documents and preserved webpages. They can include the work of major libraries alongside personal collections digitised by somebody who simply recognised that the material might otherwise disappear.

My most frequently used archive is the Internet Archive.

Its collections have supported several of the projects featured throughout my portfolio. Music discovered through the Archive has been used in my radio projects. Bootleg Broadcast uses direct video files from archived collections to populate its channels with classic television, films, seasonal specials, adverts, idents and unusual broadcasts.

The Archive contains hours of VHS and DVD recordings, sometimes preserving menus, advertisements and special features that were lost during later streaming releases. It includes unaired pilots, alternate versions, regional broadcasts, public-domain films and programmes that never received a modern commercial release.

Not every uploaded item automatically has the same copyright status, so the existence of a file does not remove the need to consider how it can legally be reused. For research and preservation, however, the scale of the material is remarkable.

The Wayback Machine adds another layer by preserving snapshots of websites across time. A page that was deleted, redesigned or abandoned years ago may still be visible through an archived copy. It is effectively a route back through internet history.

The Internet Archive was established around the idea that libraries and archives should continue preserving knowledge and culture into the digital age. Its collections extend beyond webpages into books, television news, government information, film and sound recordings.

I think of the Wayback Machine as a kind of digital Indiana Jones: travelling into ruins left by the old internet and recovering pages that would otherwise have disappeared.

Archives are valuable for personal projects, historical research, education and entertainment, but their most important purpose is preservation. Digital media can vanish surprisingly quickly. A company closes, a server fails, a licence expires or somebody decides an old piece of work no longer has commercial value.

An archive says that value is not limited to whatever can be sold today.

Tools are only as useful as the ideas behind them

None of these tools creates a finished project by itself.

AI can suggest an answer, but somebody still has to judge it. A background remover can isolate an image, but somebody must decide where it belongs. Yarn can locate a quotation, but it cannot invent the entire joke surrounding it. Canva can provide a template, but a template does not automatically become an original design.

The reason I return to these tools is that each removes a particular obstacle between an idea and its finished form.

Some help me find information. Others help me collect, edit or preserve material. Several have directly influenced the projects on this website, from YouTube thumbnails and Fallout edits to the Pi-Boy, Internet Archive scraper and Bootleg Broadcast.

The internet is increasingly dominated by a small number of enormous platforms, but beneath them there are still specialist tools, strange databases, community archives and independent websites capable of doing one thing exceptionally well.

Those are usually the corners worth saving.
