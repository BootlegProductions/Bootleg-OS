Pirate Radio

An interactive online radio with three hand-curated stations, a full boombox player and a little receiver that follows you around Bootleg OS.

The idea

I wanted a radio that felt like tuning into a place rather than opening another playlist. The full console and the small receiver in the site header share the same station, track and playback state, so the music can follow you while you browse.

Three signals to tune into

The stations take some of their spirit from the radios in Fallout: finding old recordings, giving them a place to live together and letting the listener stumble onto a song they would not have searched for by name.

89.5 FM

Pirate Oldies

Vintage popular music and broadcasts for the familiar, slightly crackly feeling of a radio from another era.

95.7 FM

Western Trails

Western-flavoured songs and atmosphere for a long drive across an imaginary wasteland.

101.4 FM

Classical Signal

Orchestral and classical recordings when the scene needs to feel grander, stranger or more dramatic.

Building the player

The interface borrows from older interactive websites, where a button or dial felt like part of an object you could operate. I wanted the boombox and header receiver to be playful, physical-looking pieces of the site, with tuning, track controls and volume rather than a plain audio element dropped into a page.

Both views use the same JavaScript station data and remember playback position. That lets the compact receiver act as an extension of the larger radio while someone moves between the gallery, projects and blog. The stations are collections of direct audio links, so individual files can be reviewed and replaced without rebuilding the interface.

The Internet Archive connectionThe Archive is an extraordinary catalogue of recordings that might otherwise be hard to find. Searching its collections has helped me discover old songs, compare versions and assemble station libraries from material preserved by other people. An Archive upload alone does not establish the rights to a recording, so each item still needs its own review before use.

This remains a growing part of Bootleg OS. I can change the station lists, add more material and keep refining the receiver as I find better recordings and ways to make the controls feel more natural.
