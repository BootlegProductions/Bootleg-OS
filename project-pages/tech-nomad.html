Tech-Nomad

A proposed mapping and community app for campers, caravanners and people exploring life off the grid.

At a glance

I acted as project manager in a virtual team exercise. The proposal defined the app, its audience, team roles, design process and the responsibilities that location and community features would bring.

Project status

This is a university concept and portfolio study. The page documents the designs and research that survive, rather than presenting a launched product.

Finding a place to stay

Tech-Nomad proposed a map of camping areas and places suitable for temporary stays, with information to help people judge whether a location was safe, clean and permitted. The idea was aimed at campers, caravanners and people interested in tiny homes or a less conventional way of living. It also tried to encourage respect for natural spaces rather than leaving litter behind.

Users would be able to post photographs and notes about places they had visited. Instead of ordinary stars, a tree-based rating system would carry reviews in the visual language of the app. The concept included a location search, map page and user profile, as shown in the surviving sketchbook.

Planning the experience

The team proposal described visual design, front-end development, data analysis and back-end roles. My project-manager role involved planning tasks, deadlines and discussions for this simulated team. The work used a Scrum-style workflow and Trello to organise what needed doing, what was in progress and what had been completed.

Personas helped define who would use the map and how comfortable they might be with the technology. The design brief called for natural colours and textures, straightforward navigation and readable location details. The original proposal named Figma for flow charts and prototypes, alongside graphics tools for the identity.

Design considerationA real version would need accurate information about where camping is allowed, careful treatment of location data and moderation of user posts. Those were identified in the proposal; the university work did not launch a live location service.

What the proposal taught me

Tech-Nomad brought product thinking and project planning together. The map was only one part of the experience: trust in the information, a useful review format and clear responsibility for user data were just as important to the design.
