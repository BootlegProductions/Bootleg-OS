Making a seventies horror short

Porcelain Man was my short film for a video editing and post-production class. I wanted the feel of a classic seventies horror story: an unsettling figure, a victim, a detective and the sense that something dreadful might be waiting just outside the frame. My girlfriend played the victim, and two friends took the roles of the detective and the Porcelain Man.

We used the places we had around us. Scenes were shot in abandoned buildings, woodland, quiet alleys and inside my apartment. Moving between those locations helped make the film feel larger than our tiny cast and crew, even though the whole production was pulled together quickly.

Filming through restrictions

Covid was happening at the time, making it difficult to organise people, locations and a conventional production schedule. We worked with whoever and whatever was available. The short was filmed and edited on my phone with simple editing and design apps, from what I remember a mixture of CapCut and Canva.

That limited setup affected the film, but it also forced me to think about framing, atmosphere and what could be suggested without elaborate equipment. The surviving costume photographs, test footage and edit clips show the process alongside the finished trailer.

What survivesThe trailer on YouTube is still available. This page also contains costume images and clips from the production, so the project can be seen as more than a single final video.

Where I would take it next

I would like to make more films in the future, with more time to plan the story, shoot and sound. Porcelain Man was a rough first experiment in taking an idea out of a presentation and actually putting people in front of a camera. It made me want to branch into other genres and mediums instead of leaving every idea as a mock-up.
